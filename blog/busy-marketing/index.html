CLARITY NOTES / MARKETING

Busy marketing isn’t always effective

There can be plenty happening in your marketing without much clarity about what any of it is actually meant to produce.

Clarity Notes #0016 min read11 August 2026By Jayden Cooke

It’s very easy to feel productive in marketing.

You’re posting on social media.
You’re sending emails.
You’re updating the website.
You’re making videos.
You’re trying a new platform.

There’s plenty happening.

But there’s an important question underneath all of that activity:

What result is it actually producing?

Because being busy with your marketing and having effective marketing are not necessarily the same thing.

Activity isn’t the same as progress

One of the things I often see is businesses continuing with marketing activity because it has simply become part of the routine.

“We post three times a week.”

“We send a newsletter every month.”

“We’re on Instagram because we need to be.”

None of those things are inherently wrong.

The problem comes when nobody can really explain what that activity is meant to achieve.

For example, you might be posting on Instagram five times a week and getting decent engagement.

Great.

But what happens next?

Are people visiting your website?

Are they joining your mailing list?

Are they enquiring?

Are they learning something that makes them more likely to buy later?

Or are you simply producing five posts a week because that’s what you’ve always done?

The same applies to email.

You might faithfully send a newsletter every month, but if you’ve never looked at what people click, what they respond to or whether the email moves anyone further along the customer journey, then the newsletter has become an activity rather than a tool.

Start with the result

A much more useful way to look at marketing is to work backwards.

Instead of asking:

“What marketing should we do?”

Ask:

“What are we trying to achieve?”

Then look at whether the activity supports that result.

A social post might exist to introduce you to new people.

An email might be designed to bring previous customers back.

A landing page might be there to turn interest into enquiries.

A case study might provide reassurance to someone who is already considering buying.

They don’t all need to create an immediate sale.

But they should have a reason to exist.

This also makes marketing easier to measure

Once you know what something is meant to do, you can decide how to measure it.

If a short video is designed to reach new people, views and watch time might matter.

If an email is designed to generate bookings, clicks and bookings are much more useful.

If a web page is designed to generate enquiries, traffic alone doesn’t tell you very much. You need to know what visitors do once they arrive.

Without the intended result, you’re left with numbers rather than insight.

And that’s where marketing can start to feel frustrating.

You’re doing plenty, but you’re still not really sure what’s working.

YOUR ACTION

Choose one marketing activity you do regularly.

It could be your newsletter, LinkedIn, Instagram, networking, your website, paid advertising or something else.

Then write down:

“The result this activity is meant to produce is…”

If you can answer that clearly, great.

If you can’t, that’s probably where your next bit of clarity needs to come from.

Because the aim isn’t to do more marketing.

It’s to understand what deserves your time, what’s creating value, and what you can stop doing simply because you’ve always done it.
